About Foundra

We're building the clearest way to start a business.

Foundra exists because starting a business in America shouldn't require a law degree. Yet today, a third of new founders open without proper licenses — not because they're careless, but because the information is fragmented, intimidating, and written for lawyers.

The problem we're solving

Every year, 5.5 million Americans file to start a new business. Most of them spend hours digging through state agency websites, federal portals, and conflicting blog posts trying to figure out what licenses, permits, and paperwork they need. Many give up. Others move forward without realizing they're operating illegally — and end up paying thousands in fines or facing closure.

Lawyers and accountants can help, but at $200–$500 per hour, they're out of reach for most first-time founders. That gap — between "I have an idea" and "I'm legally operating" — is where good ideas die.

What we built

Foundra is an AI-powered compliance navigator. You describe your business in plain English, tell us where you're starting it, and we deliver a verified checklist of every license, permit, and step you need — with direct links to official government applications.

No legalese. No 47-tab government website rabbit holes. Just clarity, in minutes.

What we believe

i.

Clarity over completeness

We'd rather give you 80% of the answer simply than 100% in legalese you can't parse.

ii.

Trust through transparency

We cite government agencies. We show our sources. We never hide fees.

iii.

Accessibility-first

Free tier always available. Premium tiers reserved for users who need depth.

iv.

Compliance, not paranoia

We help people start. We don't scare them away.

Where we're going

Today Foundra covers 14 business types across all 50 states. Tomorrow we'll add real-time license tracking, AI-powered Q&A trained on millions of compliance documents, and an expert marketplace connecting founders with vetted attorneys and accountants at flat-fee rates.

If you're a founder, an investor, or just someone who's frustrated with how hard it is to start a business in America — we'd love to hear from you.
